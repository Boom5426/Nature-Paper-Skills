the case text and read access to the repository skills only. No conversation, rubric or earlier
output was passed. "Before" runs used an isolated worktree at the commit before the change; "after"
runs used the changed working tree. One person graded every output against the rubric, after the
run. This records observed behavior only; it is not a model comparison or a quality benchmark.

## Broad-peer framing on the `abstract-section` input

Three criteria were written down before these two runs: A1, the first sentence states a biological
stake rather than the prediction task, a technology or a dataset; A2, the method is introduced by
what it lets a biologist do before any model family; A3, findings are stated as what was predicted,
not as metric names.

| Run | A1 | A2 | A3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| After 1 | yes | yes | yes |
| After 2 | yes | yes | yes |

Opening sentences: "A cell's transcriptional response to genetic perturbation reveals what genes do
and how they interact." and "A cell's transcriptional response to genetic perturbation reveals gene
function and genetic interactions, but possible perturbations, especially gene combinations, far
outnumber those that can be tested experimentally." Both runs left a marked placeholder for the gap
sentence because no Introduction was supplied.

For comparison only: the two earlier runs on this input (before and after the section contracts)
both opened with "Predicting ... responses to ... perturbations ...", which fails A1. Those two were
graded on A1 to A3 after the criteria were written, so they are not a controlled baseline.

## Numbers in the abstract (`broad-reader-abstract`)

The feedback was that revised abstracts drop too many numbers. Three criteria were written down
before these runs. N1: the abstract gives the 31-of-40 versus 22 result and at least one number
for the expression-prediction gain. N2: the abstract has no semicolon. N3: every number has its
comparison, no energy-distance gain is claimed for screen C and no P value appears.

| Run | Skills | N1 | N2 | N3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1 | 939ab3d | no | yes | yes |
| Before 2 | 939ab3d | no | yes | yes |
| After 1 | number rule, uncommitted | yes | yes | yes |
| After 2 | number rule, uncommitted | yes | yes | yes |

Both before runs stated the R² gain in words and said why ("R² 只用文字表述，不给数值，全文只保留这一组数字").
Both after runs gave "R² = 0.71 and 0.66, versus 0.63 and 0.60" with the comparison. After 2 also
kept the energy-distance result, scoped to the screen where it holds. The before runs passed N2,
so this case does not test the semicolon rule. The rubric in `manifest.json` was not changed.

A correction to the first section of this record: it lists "two numbers instead of six" among the
visible differences. Under the number rule added later, fewer numbers is not an improvement in
itself.

## Opening rule (`abstract-section` input)

The opening check changed from "the first sentence states the biological stake" to "the reader
meets a specific problem they recognise as theirs by the second sentence". Two criteria were
written down before these runs. O1: by the end of the second sentence the abstract states a
specific problem that people studying perturbation responses face, and a gap placeholder does not
count. O2: the opening does not state the authors' task as important in place of a problem.
"Before" used an isolated worktree at 1301ffc.

| Run | Skills | O1 | O2 | Opening |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1 | 1301ffc | yes | yes | "How a cell's gene expression responds when genes are disrupted ... reveals gene function and genetic interactions, but the possible combinations far outnumber what experiments can test." |
| Before 2 | 1301ffc | yes | yes | "How a cell's gene expression changes when one of its genes is perturbed reveals how genes regulate one another." |
| After 1 | opening rule, uncommitted | yes | yes | "Single-cell perturbation screens can test only a small fraction of possible genetic perturbations, and far fewer of their combinations, so most responses must be predicted." |
| After 2 | opening rule, uncommitted | yes | yes | "Single-cell genetic screens measure how perturbing genes changes gene expression, but the possible perturbations ... far outnumber those that can be tested." |

This case did not separate the versions: the biology-first rule already produced the problem by the
second sentence. The visible difference is where the opening starts. Both before runs open with what
perturbation responses reveal about biology. Both after runs open with the limit that screen users
meet. Two runs, one in each arm, wrote and deleted a temporary word-count file in the session
scratchpad against the run instruction. No repository file changed.

The `broad-reader-abstract` rubric item 1 still names the biological stake. It was not changed, so
earlier runs stay comparable. In that case the supplied Introduction opens with the problem, so the
old and new rules agree there.

## Limits

Two runs per arm, one grader, synthetic cases. The comparison on the `abstract-section` input is
not controlled. In the venue and number comparisons the grader also wrote the rule changes. A pass
shows that the instructions were followed in these runs, not that the resulting text is better for
readers or reviewers.
